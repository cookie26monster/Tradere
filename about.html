Welcome to My Story

My path back to self

Hi, I’m Tracey.

For most of my life, caring for other people has been woven into who I am.

I’ve spent around 30 years working in caring professions, including 25 years as a nurse. But caring started long before it became my career.

My aunt sustained a brain injury when I was eight years old, and although my gran and mum were her main carers, supporting my aunt has been part of my family life ever since.

Then came a period of my life where loss seemed to follow loss.

My gran’s brother died in May 2020. My gran died that December. My mum had been unwell with COPD for many years before she eventually died in May 2022.

I had an active role in caring for each of them in their illness then towards the end of their lives, while working as a nurse, caring for patients, some of whom were themselves approaching the end of life, supporting my own children and helping my mum with the care of my aunt.

When Mum died, I took over my aunt’s care fully.

For a long time, I just kept going.

Because that’s what I knew how to do……

Until I didn’t…..

The many masks we wear

Over the years I’ve worn many masks.

The nurse. The mum. The daughter. The partner. The carer. The capable one. The strong one. The one who copes. The one who looks after everyone else.

And none of those things were false. They’re all parts of me.

But when we spend enough time being who everybody else needs us to be, there’s a question we can easily forget to ask:

Who am I underneath all of that?

I certainly had.

For years I was living much of my life mechanically, functioning, doing, caring, getting through the next shift, the next responsibility, the next thing that needed me.

I was surviving my life more than consciously living it.

And my body was paying the price.

I spent years overriding what it was trying to tell me. I used food in ways that weren’t serving me. I used alcohol, antidepressants and other medications to numb what I didn’t know how to deal with. I lived for far too long in a state that felt like constant fight or flight without even realising that’s where I was.

Body, mind and spirit were all telling me something.

I just wasn’t listening.

When Mum died, something shifted.

I stopped drinking and came off my antidepressants because I knew, for me personally, that if I was going to find my way through what I was feeling, I could no longer keep numbing it.

I had to feel it.

And eventually, after years of carrying so much, I burned out.

I was signed off work for a year.

For the first time in what felt like forever, I had to stop.

And when all the doing stopped, I was left with myself.

That meant turning towards the one person I’d spent very little time caring for...

Me.

Meeting myself again

That year became about much more than recovering enough to return to work.

For perhaps the first time, I began looking at myself fully.

I had grief to process, but I also had years of patterns, beliefs, coping mechanisms and ways of treating myself that I had never really stopped long enough to examine.

I began asking questions.

Why do I react like this?

Why do I speak to myself that way?

Where did that belief come from?

Is it actually true?

Is this something I consciously chose or something I’ve simply repeated for years?

What does my body need from me?

And perhaps the biggest question of all:

Who am I when I’m not being who everybody else needs me to be?

My path back wasn’t one thing.

I went to counselling, hypnotherapy and had coaching. I done shadow work. I sat in women’s circles. I attended healing weekends and retreats. I explored breathwork, meditation, sound therapy and energy work. I went deeper into spirituality and consciousness and became increasingly fascinated by the relationship between our thoughts, beliefs, emotions, experiences and physical bodies.

Some things helped me understand.

Some helped me feel.

Some helped me release.

Some gave me practical tools.

And some simply gave me permission to stop doing and be.

Little by little, underneath all those masks, I started reconnecting with myself.

I used to live so much of my life on autopilot. Today, I try to live it much more consciously, and I say try deliberately…

I haven’t reached some permanently enlightened state where old beliefs never appear and life never gets the better of me. Far from it.

Old patterns still creep in.

I still occasionally catch that familiar voice in my head speaking to me in a way I would never speak to somebody else. I notice old reactions appearing or beliefs influencing how I’m seeing a situation.

The difference is that now I notice them.

A huge part of my own practice has become self-observation.

I observe my thoughts, my reactions, the language I use towards myself, what happens in my body and the patterns that keep repeating.

Instead of automatically becoming caught up in them, I’m increasingly able to shine a light on what’s happening and become curious about it.

Where did you come from?

Are you true?

Are you protecting me from something?

Do you still belong here?

Are you helping me become the person I want to be or sabotaging her?

Sometimes I can change the pattern.

Sometimes I’m still working on it.

And sometimes just becoming conscious of it is the first step.

I’ve come to believe that conscious living isn’t about controlling every thought or getting everything right. It’s about becoming aware enough to have a choice.

The path isn’t a straight line

Something else I’ve learned is that there is no neat, straight path back to yourself.

I’d love to tell you I did the work, learned the lessons and have floated peacefully through life ever since.

I haven’t.

Life keeps happening.

There are twists, turns, losses, relationships, responsibilities, unexpected changes and the occasional curveball that comes flying out of nowhere.

Sometimes I’m deeply connected to myself.

Sometimes I realise I’ve drifted again.

The difference now is that I know how to find my way back.

Over the years I’ve built a toolkit that I can draw from depending on what life is asking of me.

Meditation helps me find stillness and observe what’s happening within me. Breathwork can reconnect me with my body and emotions. Coaching gives me ways of questioning my thinking, beliefs and direction. Sound and energy practices offer different ways of slowing down and reconnecting.

Sometimes I need stillness.

Sometimes I need presence.

Sometimes I need movement.

Sometimes I need to question a belief.

Sometimes I need structure and a plan.

Sometimes I need to stop trying to fix anything and just allow myself to feel.

There isn’t one tool for every moment because we aren’t the same person in every moment.

And perhaps the most valuable thing I’ve learned isn’t just how to use those tools, It’s how to listen to myself well enough to recognise which one I need.

That’s something I want to bring into my work with others.

I don’t want you to need a coach every time life throws you a curveball. I want to help you understand yourself, explore different tools and gradually build your own toolkit. One that helps you navigate the twists and turns of your life without having to look outside for guidance.

A different direction

As my relationship with myself changed, I couldn’t ignore another realisation.

After 25 years, nursing no longer felt aligned with the direction I was moving in. That was difficult to admit and even more difficult to let go of. It had become part of my identity. Something I’d worked hard on and built over many years. Something I was proud of.

Nursing has given me an extraordinary education in people, health, vulnerability, life and death. I’ve sat beside people during some of the hardest moments of their lives, and those experiences will always be part of me.

But I found myself wanting to work differently.

I became increasingly interested in prevention, in what happens before we reach crisis, and in everything that surrounds our physical health that leads to dis-ease.

I wanted time to ask different questions. I wanted to do more than manage symptoms. I wanted to understand the root cause. I wanted to understand the mind-body connection.

Eventually, I realised that I could be incredibly grateful for everything nursing had given me, without it having to be where the rest of my story was written.

So after 25 years, I walked away.

And that’s how I arrived here

Sovereign Sequence wasn’t created because I think I’ve found all the answers. It grew from discovering the importance of learning how to find my own.

I’m still doing that.

I’m still peeling back layers. Still catching patterns. Still questioning beliefs. Still learning about my body, mind and consciousness. Still adapting when life changes course.

I’m still discovering who Tracey is underneath all those masks and perhaps that’s why I don’t believe my role is to tell another person who they should become.

I want to help you become curious about who you already are.

To offer some of the tools I’ve gathered along the way, ask questions you may not have thought to ask yourself and create a space where you can slow down enough to hear your own answers.

Because the goal isn’t to create a life where nothing ever knocks you off course. It’s to know yourself well enough that, when it does, you know how to begin finding your way back.

That’s my path back to self.

And if something in my story feels familiar, perhaps you’re ready to begin exploring yours.
